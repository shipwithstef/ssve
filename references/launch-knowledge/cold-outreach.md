## Email Frameworks

### PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution
**Best for:** Problem-aware but not solution-aware prospects

```
[Identify pain] → [Amplify consequences] → [Present solution + soft CTA]
```

**Example:**
> Most café owners lose $500-2000/month on dead hours. That's revenue walking out the door every Tuesday afternoon. Flash Offers fills those hours in 40 minutes — no Groupon, no strangers. Worth a look?

### BAB — Before, After, Bridge
**Best for:** Transformation-driven offers

```
[Current painful situation] → [Ideal future] → [Your product as bridge]
```

**Example:**
> Right now, your Tuesday 3pm slot sits empty while staff stands around. Imagine that slot 40% full with regulars who already love your place. Example Marketplace makes that happen with one tap.

### QVC — Question, Value, CTA
**Best for:** C-suite, brevity-focused

```
[Pain question] → [Brief value] → [Direct CTA]
```

**Example:**
> Losing money on empty afternoon slots? Flash Offers fills them with your regulars. $4.99/mo. Worth a look?

### 3C's — Compliment, Case Study, CTA
**Best for:** Agency/services outreach

```
[Genuine compliment] → [Similar success story] → [Ask]
```

**Example:**
> Love what you've done with [Business Name]. We just helped a similar café in Austin fill 15 dead hours and make $2,847 extra. Have a few ideas for you. Interested?

### Star-Story-Solution
**Best for:** Strong customer success stories

```
[Introduce character] → [Tell challenge narrative] → [Reveal results]
```

**Example:**
> Last year, Maria — a café owner in Portland — had the same problem as every café: dead hours. She started using Flash Offers. Within 30 days, she filled 15 dead hours and made $2,847 extra. Happy to share how this could work for you.

## Subject Lines

**Rules:**
- 2-4 words, lowercase
- No punctuation tricks
- Should look like it came from a colleague
- No product pitches, no urgency, no emojis

**Good examples:**
- `your hours on Google`
- `Tuesday 2pm`
- `quick question`
- `thoughts on this`

**Bad examples:**
- `Increase your revenue by 300%!`
- `🚀 Don't miss out!!!`
- `Following up on my previous email`

## Personalization Levels

### Level 1 — Basic (merge tags)
First name, company name. Table stakes, no longer differentiating. ~5% lift.

### Level 2 — Industry/segment
Industry-specific pain points. Scalable via micro-segmentation.

### Level 3 — Role-level
Challenges specific to their role and seniority.

### Level 4 — Individual (gold standard)
Specific, timely observations about that person connected to the problem you solve.

**Research signals:**
| Signal | Where to find | How to use |
|--------|---------------|------------|
| Wrong Google hours | Google Maps | "Your hours say X but you were open" |
| Low reviews | Yelp/Google | "4+ stars but few reviews" |
| New business | Google Maps | "Congrats on opening" |
| Hiring | LinkedIn | "Noticed you're hiring" |
| Recent funding | Crunchbase | "Congrats on the raise" |

## Follow-Up Sequence

### Optimal Cadence
| Touch | Day | Notes |
|-------|-----|-------|
| Initial email | 0 | Maximum personalization |
| Follow-up 1 | 3 | Different angle |
| Follow-up 2 | 7-8 | New value piece |
| Follow-up 3 | 14 | Social proof |
| Follow-up 4 | 21-28 | Breakup email |

### Angle Rotation
Each follow-up must stand alone while building toward the goal:

| Email | Angle | Purpose |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Initial | Personalized hook + core value | Introduce problem/solution |
| Follow-up 1 | Different angle, new value | Show additional benefit |
| Follow-up 2 | Social proof / case study | Build credibility |
| Follow-up 3 | New insight or resource | Demonstrate expertise |
| Follow-up 4 | Breakup — leave door open | Trigger loss aversion |

### Breakup Email
Leverages loss aversion. 10-15% response rate from cold prospects.

**Example:**
> I haven't heard back, so I'll assume now isn't the right time. Before I close the loop: we just launched geofencing — your phone detects when customers are nearby and sends them a Flash Offer automatically. If that changes things, feel free to reply. Otherwise, no hard feelings.

## What to Avoid

| Mistake | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| Too long (100+ words) | 83% fewer replies |
| Too self-focused | Signals sales pitch |
| No clear value prop | 71% ignore irrelevant emails |
| Generic templates | Detected instantly |
| Feature dumping | One proof point beats ten |
| Asking for 30-min call | "Proposing on first date" |
| Pushy language | 67% more spam flags |
| "Just checking in" | 12% drop in bookings |
| "I never heard back" | 12% drop in bookings |
| Fake urgency | Destroys trust |
