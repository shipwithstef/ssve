# Boulevard — Premium business platform for self-care brands

**URL:** https://www.joinblvd.com
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (analysis from public web — binary captures TODO via track-visuals external-anchor)
**Vertical:** local-business-saas — premium beauty / wellness (multi-location salons, med-spas)

## What it does well
- **Hero deliberately rejects the SMB visual language.** Editorial photography (high-fashion model, gallery-quality lighting), serif typography, generous whitespace — the page LOOKS like a luxury brand site, not a SaaS dashboard. Filters out price-sensitive SMB buyers and attracts premium multi-location operators.
- **Headline names a positioning, not a feature.** "The premier client experience platform" — the word "premier" is the qualifying filter. Cheap-seeking buyers leave; aspirational buyers lean in.
- **Trust bar uses RECOGNIZABLE premium-beauty brands** (Toni&Guy, Heyday, Squeeze) — peer-credibility within the premium tier specifically.
- **Demo CTA is the only CTA in the hero — no free trial.** This is intentional friction: Boulevard wants the sales-assisted motion, not self-serve, because the customer LTV justifies it.
- **Sub-hero band is a metric trio framed as outcomes ("+27% revenue per client, +33% rebooking rate").** Real data from real customers, anchoring the premium price against measurable lift.

## What it gets wrong (or trades off)
- Zero pricing transparency anywhere on the page — the demo-required wall blocks the curious browser who would have qualified themselves out at the price.
- Aspirational photography is so polished that smaller premium operators (single-location med-spa, owner-operator) may assume they're "not big enough" — segment exclusion is a feature here but it's wider than intended.

## Reusable archetype
**Name:** editorial-premium-hero
**Pattern:** Treat the landing page like a luxury brand campaign rather than a software product page. Editorial photography, serif typography, single sales-assisted CTA. Headline contains a qualifying adjective ("premier," "elite," "professional-grade") that pre-segments visitors. Outcomes shown as metric-trio, never feature lists.

## When to use this archetype
- Product is priced 3-5x above SMB-tier competitors and segmentation is the goal, not volume
- Customer acquisition cost can support sales-assisted motion; product LTV is high
- The buyer cares about being seen as a premium operator and the page acts as a status signal
