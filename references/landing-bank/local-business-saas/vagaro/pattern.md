# Vagaro — Salon, spa & fitness booking + business mgmt

**URL:** https://www.vagaro.com
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (analysis from public web — binary captures TODO via track-visuals external-anchor)
**Vertical:** local-business-saas — beauty / wellness (salons, spas, barbershops, nails)

## What it does well
- **Hero immediately offers a 30-day free trial with no card** — the offer is the primary visual element, larger than the headline. Beauty owners shop on price first; the offer kills the objection before it forms.
- **Multi-vertical headline rotates the noun.** "Software for [Salons | Spas | Barbershops | Fitness]" — the buyer sees their own category cycle through within 4 seconds, confirming "yes this is for me" without needing to navigate.
- **Hero visual is a stylized phone mockup showing the consumer-side booking flow, not the owner dashboard.** Smart move: the owner cares more about "will my clients use this?" than about admin UI.
- **Two-sided value-prop band sits below the hero.** Left: "For your business" (admin features). Right: "For your clients" (consumer app, marketplace exposure). Resolves the network-effects pitch in one screen.
- **Marketplace exposure ("List on the Vagaro marketplace, reach 22M consumers") is positioned as a free benefit, not an upsell.** Differentiates from Square Appointments / Booksy where marketplace presence is implicit.

## What it gets wrong (or trades off)
- The pricing page later contradicts the simplicity of the hero — modular add-ons (online store, payroll, branded app, forms) inflate the apparent base price; visitors who came for "$30/mo" leave when the real number is $120+.
- Vertical-rotation headline reads gimmicky on first encounter; could have been a static "for beauty + wellness" with the same effect.

## Reusable archetype
**Name:** two-sided-marketplace-hero
**Pattern:** Hero leads with the trial offer as the largest visual unit; headline rotates through served sub-verticals; hero visual shows the CONSUMER-facing experience, not the admin UI. A two-column band immediately below splits "for the business" vs "for your clients" and frames marketplace network-effects as a bundled benefit.

## When to use this archetype
- Product has both admin AND consumer-facing surfaces and you want to sell both sides at once
- A built-in consumer marketplace is a real moat vs competitors
- Buyer category spans 3-6 sub-verticals that share the same software but want to see their own name
