# Shopmonkey — Modern shop-mgmt OS for auto repair

**URL:** https://www.shopmonkey.io
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (analysis from public web — binary captures TODO via track-visuals external-anchor)
**Vertical:** local-business-saas — automotive repair shops

## What it does well
- **Hero positions explicitly against the legacy competitor.** "The Mitchell1 / R.O. Writer alternative shops actually love" — names the incumbent the buyer is currently frustrated with. Comparison-buying is the dominant intent in this vertical and the page addresses it head-on.
- **Hero visual is a clean isometric render of the work-order screen with a real VIN, real parts catalog lookup, and inspection photos attached.** Looks modern next to competitor screenshots that look like Windows 2003 UIs — the visual contrast IS the value prop.
- **Single bold CTA: "Book a demo."** No competing trial / pricing / video options. Founder thesis is that the demo is where conversion happens.
- **Sub-hero is a 6-card grid mapping legacy-shop pains to product fixes** ("Tired of double-entering customer info? → Unified customer record"). The structure mirrors how the buyer mentally lists their grievances.
- **Customer logos are independent shops with names + state ("Joe's Garage, Nashville TN").** Peer recognition for an audience that distrusts corporate brands.

## What it gets wrong (or trades off)
- The aggressive competitor-positioning means anyone who doesn't already know Mitchell1 / R.O. Writer (i.e., new shop owners) gets confused by the hero — the page assumes existing context.
- Pricing is hidden behind the demo wall; small-shop owners who can't afford enterprise SaaS leave without realizing they're priced in.

## Reusable archetype
**Name:** competitor-displacement-hero
**Pattern:** Name the legacy incumbent in the hero headline as the explicit anti-pattern ("the [Incumbent] alternative"). Use the hero visual to demonstrate visual modernity vs the dated competitor. Replace the trust bar with pain-to-fix grid that mirrors the buyer's mental complaint list.

## When to use this archetype
- One or two legacy products dominate the vertical and most prospects are actively frustrated with them
- Visual modernity is itself a credible differentiator (the legacy product genuinely looks dated)
- Sales team is willing to work demo leads — this archetype kills self-serve

## Feature mix surfaced
- Digital work orders and estimates
- Digital vehicle inspections with photos
- Parts catalog integration
- Customer communications (text estimates, approval-by-link)
- Payments (in-shop and remote)
- Inventory and parts ordering
- Technician timeclock and productivity reporting
- Multi-location consolidated reporting
