# Mews — The hospitality cloud that frees you from the front desk

**URL:** https://www.mews.com
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** b2b-saas-vertical (modern cloud hotel PMS)

## What it does well

- **Hero positions against an emotional pain, not a feature gap.** "Free your team from the front desk" — speaks to the hotelier who's tired of staff chained to a terminal. Most PMS competitors lead with features; Mews leads with the operational liberation.
- **Hero visual is a guest self-checking-in on a phone, not the staff dashboard.** Counter-intuitive: shows the guest experience, not the operator UI. Communicates "this product makes your staff invisible to guests" — the actual outcome the buyer wants.
- **"Mews vs. legacy PMS" framing throughout.** Doesn't name Opera/Protel directly but consistently uses "legacy" as the foil. Lets the buyer fill in the comparison without Mews looking aggressive — sophisticated positioning.
- **Open-API ecosystem badge in hero region.** "1,000+ integrations" sub-pill — signals the platform play vs. closed-stack competitors. Clicks through to a live integration marketplace.
- **Award badges (Hotel Tech Report #1) shown ONCE in trust bar, not repeated.** Restraint — most SaaS over-uses award badges to the point they become noise. Mews uses one, the most credible.
- **Customer story format uses chain-name + specific outcome.** "Strawberry Hotels: 35% reduction in check-in time" — operator + verb + metric + scale. Easy to mentally substitute self into.
- **Cookie banner is genuinely clean.** GDPR-required but designed in-brand, not a generic OneTrust dropdown — small detail signaling product-design culture matches what buyers want for their own guest experience.
- **"Mews Unfold" annual conference featured prominently.** Owned-event positioning — competitors can't replicate the cultural-gravity of an industry-wide gathering.
- **Open-positions link in primary nav.** Talent-marketing on the marketing site signals product-velocity (we're hiring = we're growing) and earns buyer confidence in long-term viability.

## What it gets wrong

- Pricing is fully hidden behind "Contact sales" with no indicative range — alienates SMB independent properties who need at least a ballpark to evaluate fit. A "Starts at €X/room/month" anchor would help.
- Hero video is gorgeous but heavy (~5MB above-fold); slow first paint on hospitality field connections.
- "Legacy" foil is so consistently used it risks fatigue — eventually buyers want to hear what Mews IS, not just what it isn't.

## Reusable archetype name

**Liberation-narrative hero against a generic foil** — hero leads with emotional outcome (what the operator stops doing), visual shows the end-user experience rather than the operator UI, comparison foil is generic ("legacy") rather than named to avoid aggression.

## When to use this archetype

When competing against entrenched legacy incumbents AND the buyer feels operationally trapped by the current solution. The "liberation" framing only works when the pain is genuinely emotional — for purely-functional buyers it falls flat. NOT a fit for: greenfield categories without legacy foil, purely-feature-driven buyers.

## Feature mix surfaced

- "Free your staff from the front desk": full-page hero + guest self-checkin visual
- "Open platform with 1,000+ integrations": sub-hero badge + dedicated marketplace page
- "Built for hotels, hostels, apartments — multi-property native": property-type strip mid-page

## Adoption notes

When porting this archetype, the most common mistake is copying the surface aesthetic without copying the underlying buyer-context that makes it work. Verify the buyer-segment fit BEFORE imitating the visual signature — the archetype name names the technique, not just the look. If the buyer-context match is weak, downgrade to a borrowed sub-element (one bullet) rather than the whole pattern.
