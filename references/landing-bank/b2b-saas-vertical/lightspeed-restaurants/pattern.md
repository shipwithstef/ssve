# Lightspeed Restaurants — One platform to run, manage, and grow your restaurant

**URL:** https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/restaurant
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** b2b-saas-vertical (restaurant POS, multi-location)

## What it does well

- **Hero subhead names the operator-tier explicitly.** "Built for full-service, quick-service, and multi-location restaurants" — visitor self-qualifies in 2 seconds without reading further. Reduces wrong-fit demo requests.
- **Hero visual is a split-screen iPad + back-office laptop.** Communicates the front-of-house ↔ back-office unification that's the core differentiator vs. single-device competitors (Toast handheld, Square iPad-only).
- **"Switch from Toast/Square/Aloha" anchor link in hero.** Direct competitor naming in primary nav signals confidence and intercepts comparison-shopping traffic. Each link goes to a dedicated vs. page.
- **Customer logo strip is grouped by cuisine type.** Pizza chains together, fine-dining together, QSR together — visitor sees peers in their own segment, not random hospitality logos.
- **ROI calculator embedded above the fold on mobile.** Sliders for "# of locations" and "# of covers/night" produce a personalized "you'd save $X/year" output — turns abstract value prop into a numeric anchor before any demo ask.
- **Pricing page hides exact prices behind "Talk to sales" but shows feature inclusion clearly.** Tradeoff: loses self-serve buyers but qualifies multi-location ones. Honest about the buying motion.
- **Footer includes regional/language selector first, not last.** Lightspeed is global; making this prominent reduces wrong-region traffic bouncing into US-only flows.
- **Hardware bundle photographed in actual restaurant settings.** iPad + receipt printer + cash drawer staged on a bar top (not white background) — visitor sees the install context, not abstract product shots.
- **Customer-success-team photo block on enterprise sub-page.** Real named humans with restaurant-industry backgrounds — signals "you'll work with people who know your business."

## What it gets wrong

- The hero CTA says "Get a demo" with no secondary "See features" path — visitors who aren't yet demo-ready leave. A "Watch 90-second tour" video CTA would catch the exploratory traffic.
- Page weight is heavy (~4MB above-fold) due to autoplay product video — slow on field connections (where restaurant operators often browse).
- Acquisitions of Upserve/Vend created sub-brand confusion — visitors searching for old brand names land on Lightspeed pages without clear continuity messaging.

## Reusable archetype name

**Multi-tier-vertical hero with competitor-switching shortcuts** — hero immediately names every operator sub-tier the product serves, primary nav includes "switch from <competitor>" links to intercept comparison shoppers, customer proof is grouped by sub-segment for peer matching.

## When to use this archetype

When the product serves multiple distinct sub-segments within one vertical (full-service vs QSR vs multi-location) AND faces 3+ named direct competitors with high comparison-shopping behavior. The vs. pages are load-bearing infrastructure. NOT a fit for: single-segment products, products without name-brand competitors.

## Feature mix surfaced

- "One platform: front-of-house, back-office, payments, inventory": hero headline + 4-icon strip below
- "Built specifically for full-service / QSR / multi-location": hero subhead + tier-specific sub-pages
- "Switch from Toast/Square/Aloha — we'll do the migration": competitor link strip in hero + dedicated vs. pages

## Adoption notes

When porting this archetype, the most common mistake is copying the surface aesthetic without copying the underlying buyer-context that makes it work. Verify the buyer-segment fit BEFORE imitating the visual signature — the archetype name names the technique, not just the look. If the buyer-context match is weak, downgrade to a borrowed sub-element (one bullet) rather than the whole pattern.
