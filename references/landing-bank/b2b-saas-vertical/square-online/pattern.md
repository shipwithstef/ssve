# Square Online — Build a free online store that syncs with your in-person sales

**URL:** https://squareup.com/us/en/online-store
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** b2b-saas-vertical (small-business commerce / website builder)

## What it does well

- **Hero leads with "free" and a concrete output.** Headline pairs a price anchor ("free online store") with a buyer-relevant outcome ("sync online and in-person sales") — addresses the omnichannel pain point in one breath instead of pitching abstract "ecommerce platform."
- **Two-column hero with a real storefront preview, not a stock template gallery.** Right column shows an actual rendered store (food/retail) with cart drawer expanded — visitor sees the artifact they'll get, not just promise of it.
- **Sub-hero "what comes with free" specificity.** Bulleted list under the CTA enumerates exactly what the free tier includes (free hosting, SSL, unlimited products, social-media selling). Removes the common SaaS skepticism: "what's the catch with free?"
- **Workflow-by-vertical sub-pages teased from hero.** "I run a restaurant / retail store / service business" cards send each visitor to a vertical-tailored landing — feels personalized despite being a single template.
- **Side-by-side comparison vs. Shopify and Wix lower on page.** Comparison table uses Square's own pricing strength (free tier, no transaction fees on POS) as the wedge. Anchors price perception before the visitor leaves to compare elsewhere.
- **Trust bar uses real US small-business names with city tags.** "Sister's Donuts — Brooklyn, NY" reads authentic vs. abstract Fortune-500 logos; matches how solo operators evaluate peer credibility.
- **Mobile sticky CTA appears at scroll position ~70%, not on load.** Above-fold attention is preserved; the CTA reappears once the visitor has consumed value props.
- **Template gallery is one click from hero.** Reduces empty-state objection — visitor can preview a working store template before committing to setup.
- **"What's included" sub-page goes deeper than competitors.** Each free-tier feature has a screenshot + plain-English explainer — eliminates "I don't understand what I'm getting" friction.

## What it gets wrong

- The "free vs. paid" tier transition is buried — visitor must scroll deep to learn when the free tier becomes Plus or Premium and why. Anchoring the upgrade reasons higher would reduce surprise-paywall churn.
- Hero CTA reads "Get started" — generic. "Build my free store" would mirror the headline promise more concretely.
- Square ecosystem-lock isn't disclosed — visitors don't realize the "free" anchor depends on using Square Payments at standard rates.

## Reusable archetype name

**Free-tier hero with itemized inclusion list** — headline anchors on "free," sub-hero enumerates exactly what's included, comparison table positioned as the wedge against more expensive incumbents, vertical-tailored sub-landing cards branch by buyer self-identification.

## When to use this archetype

When the product has a genuinely usable free tier AND competes against well-known paid incumbents (Shopify, Wix, Squarespace). The "free" anchor is load-bearing — without it, this archetype collapses. NOT a fit for: enterprise sales-led products, trial-only freemium with hard 14-day cliff, or products where free tier is intentionally crippled.

## Feature mix surfaced

- "Free online store with no monthly fee": full-page hero headline + persistent CTA
- "Sync online and in-person sales automatically": hero subhead + dedicated mid-page section with POS handoff diagram
- "Sell on Instagram, TikTok, Google in one click": social-commerce strip below hero with platform logos

## Adoption notes

When porting this archetype, the most common mistake is copying the surface aesthetic without copying the underlying buyer-context that makes it work. Verify the buyer-segment fit BEFORE imitating the visual signature — the archetype name names the technique, not just the look. If the buyer-context match is weak, downgrade to a borrowed sub-element (one bullet) rather than the whole pattern.
