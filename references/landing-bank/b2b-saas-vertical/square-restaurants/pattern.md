# Square for Restaurants — POS that grows with you

**URL:** https://squareup.com/us/en/restaurants (or current Square Restaurants page)
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** b2b-saas-vertical (restaurants)

## What it does well

- **Asymmetric hero — text left, dashboard mockup right.** Headline + sub + dual CTA on the left half; right half is a high-fidelity Square Restaurants dashboard mockup showing actual restaurant data (table-map view + active orders). Visual proof, not just claim.
- **Headline names the buyer outcome, not the product.** "Run a better restaurant" beats "Square Restaurants POS Software". Buyer-outcome framing is rare in B2B SaaS — most companies headline their product name first.
- **Free-tier offer in the hero.** "Free hardware kit when you sign up" — concrete, easy to act on, doesn't require sales contact. Reduces decision friction at the most important moment.
- **Sticky bottom-bar CTA on scroll.** As soon as the hero scrolls out of view, a slim sticky bar appears with "Get started — free" CTA. Always one click to convert.
- **Comparison table vs Toast/TouchBistro/Lightspeed.** Square owns the comparison framing rather than letting visitors leave to compare. Honest about where Square loses (some advanced inventory features), wins on simplicity + price.
- **Customer story tile uses video.** A 30-second clip of a real owner saying what they switched FROM and why. Higher trust than written quotes; works as a low-cost testimonial that scales across the marketing site.

## What it gets wrong

- The dashboard mockup looks plausibly real but is actually a designed mockup, not a real screenshot. If a savvy buyer notices, trust drops. Better: real screenshot with light pixel-cleanup vs full mockup.
- Pricing tiers use cute names ("Free", "Plus", "Premium") rather than what they include. Forces a click to find out what tier you need.

## Reusable archetype name

**Asymmetric hero with embedded dashboard mockup** — text+CTA left, product visualization right. Headline frames buyer outcome, not product. Free-tier or low-friction starter offer in the hero. Sticky CTA on scroll.

## When to use this archetype

Visual product (UI-heavy SaaS) + competitive market (multiple alternatives) + has a free or low-commitment tier. The asymmetric hero gives the dashboard room to breathe at desktop without sacrificing the value prop.
