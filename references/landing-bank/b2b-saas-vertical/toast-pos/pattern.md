# Toast POS — All-in-one restaurant platform that pays for itself

**URL:** https://pos.toasttab.com (or current Toast marketing root)
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** b2b-saas-vertical (restaurants)

## What it does well

- **Hero shows the actual product in restaurant context.** Not a stock laptop on a wood desk — a real Toast handheld device in a server's hand mid-shift, with kitchen blur in the background. Visitors instantly understand "this is a tool that runs my floor."
- **Workflow-cards under the hero.** Three cards: "Take orders faster", "Pay your team", "Grow online sales". Each card is a daily-workflow noun for restaurant operators, not a feature noun.
- **Specific dollar metrics in the hero subhead.** "Restaurants on Toast see X% more orders / save Y hours/week." Numbers come from Toast's own customer data; cited at the bottom of the page in fine print.
- **Trust bar uses real recognizable independents.** Local-favorites that the visitor's own customers might know, not Fortune-500 logos. Builds peer-credibility, not aspirational-credibility.
- **Pricing page is per-location, not per-user.** Matches how restaurants actually buy software; eliminates the "wait, do I need 8 seats?" friction.
- **Mobile hero is taller and tighter.** On a 6.7" phone the hero collapses the workflow-cards into a swipeable carousel; product image is centered and cropped tighter so it reads at glance.

## What it gets wrong

- The "Get a demo" CTA dominates over "See pricing" in the hero. For solo-operator restaurants (sub-$1M annual), they want to self-serve; the demo-gate adds friction. A secondary "Try free for 14 days" alternative would help.
- Footer is huge — over-designed sitemap that duplicates the top nav. Adds page weight and isn't load-bearing.

## Reusable archetype name

**Product-hero with workflow-cards** — hero shows the actual product in user-context (not abstract); below the hero, 3 cards each named after a daily-workflow verb (not a feature noun). Trust bar uses peer-recognizable customer names. Pricing model matches how the vertical actually buys.

## When to use this archetype

The product has a strong physical/contextual presence (POS, handheld, kiosk, instrument) and the buyer is operations-focused. NOT a fit for: pure-software backoffice tools (payroll, HR), abstract platforms (Stripe, Twilio), consumer-facing apps.
