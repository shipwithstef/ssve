# Resy for Restaurants — Reservation platform built by hospitality people

**URL:** https://resy.com/business (or current Resy for Restaurants page)
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** b2b-saas-vertical (restaurants — reservations)

## What it does well

- **Founder-quote hero.** Hero headline IS a quote from a recognized chef/operator about what Resy unlocked for their restaurant. Personal. Specific. The reader knows immediately this isn't generic SaaS — it's hospitality-tech made BY hospitality people.
- **Concrete metric in the subhead.** "Used by 16,000+ restaurants worldwide, processing X million covers monthly". Specific enough to feel real, vague enough to not lock Resy in to outdated numbers (and generally credible at the order of magnitude).
- **Vertical-specific trust signals.** Logos shown are James Beard winners and Michelin restaurants, not generic enterprise logos. For a fine-dining operator, this is aspirational — the page is saying "you'd be in this peer group."
- **Pricing is bundle-first.** Single all-inclusive monthly fee, not per-cover or per-seat. Eliminates the "what does this REALLY cost at my volume" anxiety. Restaurants hate variable-cost SaaS.
- **Demo CTA, not signup.** Recognizes that fine-dining operators are high-touch buyers — they want to talk to a person who understands their world. Doesn't push self-serve where it'd feel cheap.
- **"For restaurants by restaurants" tagline near footer.** Social proof done in 5 words; reinforces the founder-credibility from the hero.

## What it gets wrong

- The page is image-heavy and slow on mobile. LCP > 3s on a real LTE connection. For a sales-led B2B page that's tolerable, but it's a missed easy win.
- The pricing page is vague — "Contact us for pricing." For restaurants outside their primary fine-dining segment (casual, neighborhood spots), this kills lead-gen. Better: explicit "starts at $X/month" anchor.

## Reusable archetype name

**Founder-quote hero with peer-aspirational trust bar** — hero headline is a quote from someone the buyer respects in their own world. Trust bar uses peer-aspirational customer logos (the buyer wants to be in their company). Bundle pricing eliminates variable-cost anxiety. Demo-only CTA recognizes high-touch sales motion.

## When to use this archetype

Vertical SaaS targeting a craft/professional buyer who values peer-credibility over cost-savings. Fine-dining, boutique fitness, custom-build, design services. NOT a fit for: high-volume self-serve verticals (small-shop POS, small-team CRM) — those buyers want self-serve pricing visibility.
