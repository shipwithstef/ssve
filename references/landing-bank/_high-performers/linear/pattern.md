# Linear — The issue tracker built for high-performance teams

**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** high-performer-cross-sector (sharp-utilitarian-dev-tool)

## What it does well

- **Hero subhead names the foil directly via comparative speed claim.** Linear historically leaned on "faster than Jira" framing — competitor-naming subheads earn instant relevance with the exact buyers actively considering migration.
- **Pixel-perfect product screenshot in hero, no marketing decoration.** No floating gradient blobs, no abstract dashboard mock — the actual app UI at full fidelity. Communicates "product is the marketing" — implicit confidence signal.
- **Keyboard-shortcut hints scattered through scroll.** Lower sections show actual `Cmd+K` chips and shortcut overlays in product visuals — speaks the dev-power-user language without saying "we love power users."
- **Type system is one-font, two-weights, one-color (near-white on near-black).** Visual restraint reinforces the product's restraint-driven UX. Marketing aesthetic is the product aesthetic.
- **Customer logos are tier-1 modern tech (Vercel, Ramp, Cash App, Mercury).** Peer-aspiration logos for the dev-tool buyer demographic — not enterprise logos, modern-tech-darling logos.
- **Animated scroll-driven product demo (cycle: triage → assign → ship).** Each scroll position advances a single workflow step in the product UI; visitor sees the actual happy-path through the product without playing a video.
- **Pricing page: clear free tier, $8/$14 per-user steps, no "Contact us" tier above the visible top.** Self-serve all the way through enterprise — matches the bottom-up adoption motion.
- **Changelog is treated as marketing surface, not afterthought.** Each release entry has its own polished page with screenshots and animation — communicates product velocity in a way that feels editorial rather than promotional.
- **Negative space is load-bearing.** Each section has 200+ vertical pixels of breathing room around it; the page reads as "we have nothing to hide" rather than the cram-everything-above-fold compulsion most SaaS pages exhibit.

## What it gets wrong

- Page assumes prior context — visitors who don't know what Jira/Linear/issue-trackers ARE will bounce because no plain-English explainer exists above the product screenshot.
- Onboarding/setup is implied but never visualized — the "5 minute setup" claim isn't backed with a concrete flow.
- "For high-performance teams" tagline reads as exclusionary to early-stage teams who'd benefit most — implicit signal that Linear is for "real" teams creates friction for solo/small-team buyers.

## Reusable archetype name

**Sharp-utility hero with product-as-marketing fidelity** — hero shows pixel-exact product UI with no decoration, type/color restraint mirrors product UX, scroll-driven product demo replaces video, competitor-naming subhead earns the comparison-shopper.

## When to use this archetype

When the product is genuinely beautiful AND the buyer is a high-context power user who values restraint. The "product is the marketing" gambit fails if the product UI is mediocre — the screenshot has to actually impress. NOT a fit for: enterprise sales-led products, low-context buyers who need explainer-first pages.

## Feature mix surfaced

- "Built for the way modern software teams work": hero headline + UI-screenshot positioning
- "Keyboard-first, fast as thought": shortcut overlays + Cmd+K palette demos throughout scroll
- "Roadmaps, projects, cycles in one tool": three-screenshot vertical strip mid-page

## Adoption notes

When porting this archetype, the most common mistake is copying the surface aesthetic without copying the underlying buyer-context that makes it work. Verify the buyer-segment fit BEFORE imitating the visual signature — the archetype name names the technique, not just the look. If the buyer-context match is weak, downgrade to a borrowed sub-element (one bullet) rather than the whole pattern.
