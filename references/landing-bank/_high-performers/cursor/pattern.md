# Cursor — The AI code editor built to make you extraordinarily productive

**URL:** https://cursor.com
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** high-performer-cross-sector (bold-statement-typography)

## What it does well

- **Hero typography is oversized to the edge of the viewport.** Headline at ~120px on desktop; word-count is minimal (8-10 words). Treats type as the hero visual, not as a label for one. Demands attention through scale.
- **Subhead delivers a single claim, no qualifiers.** "The AI code editor" — definite article asserting category leadership. Fragile if disputed; powerful when it lands.
- **Hero has a single demo video that loops a tab-completion sequence.** Shows the product's killer interaction (AI suggests, user accepts via Tab) repeated in 8-second loops. Visitor sees the magic before scrolling.
- **Customer-quote tiles use named individuals from named companies.** "Lead engineer at OpenAI: 'Cursor is so good I'd pay 10x'" — individual + role + company + price-elasticity quote. Specific quotes beat company logos for AI-skeptical developers.
- **Pricing page is intentionally simple: Free / Pro $20 / Business $40.** Three tiers, three numbers, no usage-meter complexity. Matches the "extraordinarily productive" promise — buying friction matches product friction (low).
- **Comparison vs. GitHub Copilot is explicit on a sub-page.** Names the dominant incumbent and positions on agentic capability + multi-file edits where Cursor wins.
- **Download button is the only primary CTA in nav.** No "book demo" — signals self-serve confidence and removes choice paralysis.
- **Forked-from-VS-Code lineage acknowledged transparently.** Cursor doesn't hide its VS Code origins — owns it as "all your VS Code extensions work, plus AI." Reduces switching friction objection.
- **Agent mode demo video below hero is the second showpiece.** Shows multi-step autonomous coding — escalates the product narrative from "better autocomplete" to "agentic coding partner" without losing the simpler hero claim.

## What it gets wrong

- The "extraordinarily productive" claim is unbacked by quantitative proof above the fold. A "developers ship X% faster" stat with citation would fortify the claim.
- The bold typography reads as confidence to senior engineers but as marketing-bluster to skeptics — A/B test risk.
- Privacy/data-handling concerns (does Cursor train on my code?) aren't addressed in hero region — known objection for enterprise buyers.

## Reusable archetype name

**Bold-statement typography with single-claim subhead** — headline scaled to viewport edge with minimal word count, subhead asserts category leadership in one definite-article sentence, killer-interaction loop replaces feature explanation, three-tier pricing with three numbers matches product simplicity.

## When to use this archetype

When the product has a genuine "obvious-on-demo" killer interaction AND the team has earned the confidence to make a category-defining claim. Bold typography demands product confidence — if the product is mid, this archetype amplifies the disappointment. NOT a fit for: products requiring nuanced explanation, products in crowded undifferentiated categories.

## Feature mix surfaced

- "The AI code editor": full-viewport hero headline
- "Tab to accept AI suggestions across your whole file": looping demo video showing multi-line accept
- "Chat with your codebase, edit across files, agent mode": three-feature scroll strip with mini-demos

## Adoption notes

When porting this archetype, the most common mistake is copying the surface aesthetic without copying the underlying buyer-context that makes it work. Verify the buyer-segment fit BEFORE imitating the visual signature — the archetype name names the technique, not just the look. If the buyer-context match is weak, downgrade to a borrowed sub-element (one bullet) rather than the whole pattern.
