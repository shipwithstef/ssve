# Tana — A different kind of workspace, built on the supertag

**URL:** https://tana.inc
**Captured:** 2026-04-27 (pattern.md only; hero.png/.webm TODO-capture)
**Sector:** high-performer-cross-sector (calm-minimal-data-tool)

## What it does well

- **Hero leads with a novel concept ("supertag") rather than a competitor wedge.** Most note-tool competitors position against Notion/Roam/Obsidian — Tana defines a new primitive instead. Risky but earns category-creation territory if it lands.
- **Hero visual is a calm structured-outline screenshot showing supertag-typed nodes.** No flashy animation — a single still UI demonstrating the "structure emerges from typing" claim. Communicates the product's calm/contemplative aesthetic.
- **Subhead defines the primitive in one sentence.** "Supertags turn your notes into a database, automatically." Definition-as-positioning — visitor understands what they're evaluating without external context.
- **Use-case scroll cards: PKM, project management, CRM, knowledge base.** Same primitive (supertag), four use cases — communicates flexibility through concrete framing rather than abstract "build anything" claim.
- **Customer/user stories use individual power-users, not company logos.** Tana's earliest audience was the PKM-influencer crowd (Tiago Forte, Maggie Appleton-tier) — stories from named individuals build cred in that subculture.
- **Waitlist + invite system creates exclusivity narrative.** Until recently, Tana was invite-only — the friction generated FOMO and cultivated a tighter community. Now mostly open but the cultural memory persists.
- **Calm color palette: white, near-black, single accent.** Visual restraint mirrors the "structured thinking" product positioning — aesthetic is the marketing.
- **AI features integrated into supertag primitive, not bolted on.** Tana AI extends supertags (auto-tag, auto-summarize, auto-link) rather than appearing as a separate "AI" feature — positions AI as deepening the existing model.
- **Demo videos use real PKM workflows from real users.** Not staged tutorials — captures of actual researchers/writers using Tana for their work. Authenticity is the proof.

## What it gets wrong

- The "supertag" novel-primitive gambit risks being unparseable to mainstream buyers — visitors who don't already know Roam/Tana/Logseq concepts bounce because the value prop requires PKM-context to evaluate.
- Pricing isn't visible from hero nav — for a tool competing with free/cheap incumbents (Obsidian, Notion free), pricing transparency would help.
- Marketing site activity has been intermittent through 2025-2026 — some sub-pages feel under-maintained vs. the homepage.
- Mobile experience has historically lagged desktop — limits the on-the-go capture use case competitors (Apple Notes, Bear) own.

## Reusable archetype name

**Novel-primitive hero with definition-as-positioning** — hero defines a new product primitive rather than positioning against an incumbent, subhead delivers the primitive's definition in one sentence, calm visual restraint mirrors the contemplative product aesthetic, use-case cards demonstrate flexibility through concrete framings.

## When to use this archetype

When the product genuinely introduces a new primitive AND the target audience is high-context power-users who appreciate novelty. The "novel-primitive" gambit fails for mainstream audiences who need familiar reference points. NOT a fit for: mainstream-buyer products, products that are incremental improvements on existing primitives.

## Feature mix surfaced

- "A different kind of workspace, built on the supertag": full-page hero novel-primitive headline
- "Supertags turn your notes into a database, automatically": subhead definition + structured-outline visual
- "Use it for PKM, projects, CRM, or knowledge base": four-use-case scroll cards mid-page

## Adoption notes

When porting this archetype, the most common mistake is copying the surface aesthetic without copying the underlying buyer-context that makes it work. Verify the buyer-segment fit BEFORE imitating the visual signature — the archetype name names the technique, not just the look. If the buyer-context match is weak, downgrade to a borrowed sub-element (one bullet) rather than the whole pattern.
