## 1. Mechanism (factual)

### Registration and System Access
Before accessing the EIT Urban Mobility NetSuite grant management platform, applicants must execute a two-step registration process:
1. **PIC Generation**: Register the legal entity on the EU Funding & Tender Opportunities portal to obtain a 9-digit Participant Identification Code (PIC).
2. **PIF Submission**: Complete the Partner Information Form (PIF) on the EIT Urban Mobility NetSuite platform using the newly generated PIC.
3. **Processing Delay**: NetSuite registration validation and credential generation is not instantaneous; EIT Urban Mobility requires up to **two (2) working days** to process the PIF and issue login credentials.

### Technical Failures and Submission Support
If an applicant fails to submit a proposal due to a technical error exclusively attributable to the EIT Urban Mobility NetSuite platform, a strict remediation protocol applies:
*   **Notification Window**: The applicant must email `contact-361d8a4c76@example.invalid` within **three (3) calendar days** after the call closure. Complaints submitted after this 72-hour window are categorically rejected.
*   **Evidence Requirements**: The complaint must include the proposal ID number, a clear description of the issue, and **time-stamped screenshots** proving the platform malfunction.
*   **Exclusions**: Failures related to the applicant's local equipment, internet connection, computer configuration, or misunderstanding of the Call Manual/Guidelines for Applicants do not qualify for remediation.
*   **Verification and Extension**: The PMO verifies the claim against internal IT logs. If the technical failure is validated, the applicant is granted an explicit **24-hour extension** to complete the submission.

### Admissibility and Eligibility Rectification
For proposals that fail the initial admissibility or eligibility checks due to missing information or obvious clerical errors specifically linked to the Partner Information Form (PIF):
*   **Correction Window**: Applicants are granted **five (5) calendar days** from the receipt of the official notification to correct and resubmit the PIF.
*   **Adjustments**: Additional time may be granted proportionally if the window intersects with public holidays or weekends, at the discretion of the Project Leader.
*   **Penalty**: Failure to respond adequately within this timeframe results in the proposal remaining ineligible and being permanently discarded.

### Formal Appeal Procedures
Applicants have the right to appeal decisions regarding both (a) admissibility/eligibility rejections and (b) final evaluation results.
*   **Timeline**: All appeals must be lodged within **seven (7) calendar days** of receiving the official EIT Urban Mobility notification or the Summary Evaluation Report (SER).
*   **Admissibility/Eligibility Appeals**: Can be filed if the applicant believes the rejection was procedurally incorrect.
*   **Evaluation Appeals Constraint**: Disagreement with the evaluators' subjective scoring is explicitly excluded as a valid ground for appeal. An evaluation appeal is only admissible if the applicant can demonstrate that a specific comment in the SER **clearly and demonstrably contradicts** the factual information provided in the submitted proposal text.

## 2. Analysis (expert commentary)

### The Registration Bottleneck Trap
The requirement for up to two working days to process the Partner Information Form (PIF) creates a significant friction point for last-minute applicants. Unlike the EU Funding & Tender Portal, which generates a PIC almost instantly via automated validation, the EIT Urban Mobility NetSuite platform requires manual or batch-processed ingestion of the PIF. Startups discovering the call 48 hours before the cut-off effectively cannot apply, as they will be locked out of the submission portal while awaiting credentials. This structural latency acts as a shadow filter for applicant organizational maturity.

### Burden of Proof on Technical Failures
The three-calendar-day window for reporting technical failures is nominally generous, but the evidentiary burden is extraordinarily high. The mandate for "time-stamped screenshots" combined with "internal IT log verification" means applicants who experience client-side rendering issues (e.g., JavaScript failures on Safari, which the guidelines explicitly warn against) will lose their appeals. EIT Urban Mobility explicitly assumes zero liability for browser incompatibility or user-side networking timeouts.

### The Near-Impossibility of Evaluation Appeals
The 7-calendar-day appeal window for evaluations is rigidly enforced, but the actual substantive bar for a successful appeal is what makes the mechanism notable. By restricting evaluation appeals strictly to cases where the SER "clearly contradicts the information provided in the proposal," EIT Urban Mobility insulates its Investment Committee and External Expert Evaluators (EEEs) from qualitative disputes. An applicant cannot argue that the evaluators misunderstood the market size or undervalued the technology; they can only appeal if, for instance, the SER claims "the team lacks a CTO" while the proposal explicitly listed a CTO in the Team Composition section. This reduces the appeal process from a re-evaluation to a factual audit.
