**Eligibility Checklist**
To pass the strict admissibility and eligibility checks, applicants must quantitatively and qualitatively meet all of the following criteria:
*   **Geographic Establishment:** The legal entity must be established in an EU Member State or a Third Country associated with Horizon Europe (UK and Switzerland are eligible subject to specific conditions).
*   **Active Fundraising Round:** The startup must be currently engaged in a bona fide, active pre-seed, seed, or Series A fundraising round. The round must comprise primary equity or equity-linked issuance (pure secondary share sales or purely non-dilutive rounds like grants do not qualify).
*   **Target Market:** Proposed innovations must explicitly target the Urban Mobility market (e.g., Urban Logistics, Public Transport, Mobility Data Management, Electrification/Alternative Fuel, Health & Mobility).
*   **Re-application Rules:** A startup cannot apply to three consecutive cut-offs. If a startup submits applications to two consecutive cut-offs and is rejected, it must skip at least one subsequent cut-off before reapplying.
*   **Current Portfolio Exclusion:** The startup must not currently be part of the EIT Urban Mobility equity portfolio. (Defined as: Signed/executed IA or SAFE with completed share transfer; Signed IA/SAFE pending execution but within the longstop date; Signed SAFE pending execution even if the longstop date has passed).
*   **Pre-Money Valuation Cap:** The company's pre-money valuation for the current active round must strictly not exceed €50,000,000.
*   **Founder Equity Threshold:** Individual founders and executive team members must jointly hold strictly greater than 40% (>40%) of the company’s equity *prior* to the current fundraising round.
*   **Technology Readiness:** The startup must possess at least a functioning prototype, pilot, or Minimum Viable Product (MVP). Pure ideation-stage companies are ineligible.

**Hungarian Entities Restrictions**
Due to EU Council measures protecting the EU budget (effective 15 December 2022), Hungarian public interest trusts and their affiliated universities face strict participation and funding restrictions under this call and all EIT Urban Mobility instruments.

## 2. Analysis (expert commentary)

**Co-investor Bottleneck**
EIT Urban Mobility operates strictly as a co-investor and will not lead funding rounds (barring extreme exceptions). It deploys capital on a *pari-passu* basis alongside accredited private investors. For applicants, this creates a structural bottleneck: success in this call is completely interdependent on securing private VC or angel backing to price the round and set the terms (via priced equity, SAFEs, or convertible notes). A startup seeking EIT UM as a solo savior for a struggling round will be rejected under the "exclusion criteria" for failing to offer a customary market-term round. EIT UM can invest before, during, or after round closes, but the existence of a lead investor's terms is the de facto gating mechanism.

**Bulgarian Regional Innovation Scheme (RIS) 25% Co-funding Advantage**
Of the €14M total indicative funding allocated for 2026–2028, approximately €4.5M (32.1%) is heavily ring-fenced for startups in countries covered by the EIT Regional Innovation Scheme (RIS). Notably, Spain and Israel have been explicitly excluded from RIS eligibility for this specific call due to the maturity of their ecosystems. For emerging tech hubs like Bulgaria, this creates a massive structural advantage—essentially functioning as a 25%+ co-funding arbitrage. Because European capital markets heavily index toward Western Europe, EIT UM strategically acts as the "initial non-local investor" for RIS-based startups, aggressively de-risking Bulgarian mobility startups for follow-on Western European VCs.

**Founder Control Guardrails and Capitalization Dilution Dynamics**
The hard requirement that founders and executive team members must hold >40% equity pre-round serves as a rigorous anti-zombie guardrail. EIT UM is deploying public/institutional capital and systematically refuses to inject liquidity into ventures burdened by broken cap tables (e.g., spin-offs where a university or early angel took 70% of the equity). This 40% floor ensures that management retains sufficient economic incentive to drive the company to an exit. Coupled with the €50M valuation ceiling, these guardrails force EIT UM's capital explicitly into the most vulnerable but high-growth trajectory stages (Pre-Seed to Series A), protecting the fund from overpaying in late-stage momentum rounds while demanding heavy operational commitment from founders.
