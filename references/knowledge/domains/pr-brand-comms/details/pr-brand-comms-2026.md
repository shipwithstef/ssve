## 1. Earned Media / PR Strategy

### Framework: PESO Model `[STABLE]`
The Paid–Earned–Shared–Owned (PESO) framework governs how integrated comms campaigns distribute effort. Effective launch campaigns fire all four channels in coordination: owned (blog, site) seeds the narrative; earned (press) amplifies credibility; shared (social, influencer) extends reach; paid (promoted posts, sponsored content) closes the awareness gap. The critical discipline is ensuring brand message house consistency across all four — a single voice, four distribution channels.

### Press Release Pickup: Why 25% ≠ 3% `[VOLATILE]`
The 3% wire-only pickup rate vs. 25% with active outreach is the key number explaining why wire distribution alone is insufficient. The difference is relationship-first outreach: pre-warming a journalist with a tip before the full release arrives, personalizing the relevance angle, and including a quote and multimedia asset. Data-driven angles with proprietary research achieve the highest single-angle pickup rate at 47% — this is because journalists can hang a unique narrative on it that they can't find elsewhere. Source: PRLab 2026, Instant Press 2026.

### Optimal Pitch Mechanics `[VOLATILE]`
- **Length**: ≤200 words has highest response correlation. Every word above 200 reduces response probability.
- **Day/time**: Tuesday–Wednesday, 10 am–12 pm local journalist time. Avoid Monday (catch-up mode) and Friday afternoon (thinning newsroom).
- **Off-the-hour send**: Send at 8:12 or 2:40, not 8:00 or 3:00 — inbox timing competition is real.
- **Subject line**: Treat as headline. 50 characters or fewer. Specific, not clever.
- **Follow-up**: One follow-up is acceptable for 62% of journalists globally (69% US). More than one is spam territory. Source: Sword and the Script global survey, April 2026.

### Why 86% of Pitches Fail `[VOLATILE]`
The top rejection cause is lack of relevance (86%). This is not a volume problem — it is a targeting problem. Common failure modes: pitching a tech journalist on a lifestyle product, pitching at the wrong beat stage (e.g., post-funding to a seed-round reporter), and pitching without naming the story angle the journalist is actively working on. Fix: read the last 5 articles by each journalist before writing one word of the pitch.

### Earned Media ROI & EMV Methodology `[VOLATILE — methodology contested]`
The $5.94/dollar EMV figure is widely cited but methodology-dependent. The number is directionally useful; do not present it as precise. The AMEC (Barcelona Principles 3.0) recommends moving away from AVE (advertising value equivalency) entirely. A credible EMV calculation should include: impression count × CPM for the publication tier + engagement count × cost-per-engagement + authority multiplier (domain authority score) + sentiment adjustment (negative mentions reduce value). Report the methodology alongside the number or don't report the number at all. Source: AMEC; Muck Rack; Launchmetrics 2025.

## 2. Crisis Communication Playbooks

### Golden Hour Doctrine `[VOLATILE — response-time norms shift with platform speed]`
The "golden hour" is the first 60 minutes after a crisis is detected. This is not aspirational — it is a hard operational standard in 2025-2026. Research from the Institute for Public Relations shows organizations acknowledging within 60 minutes retain 80% more audience trust than those responding after 24 hours. The 2025 MIT Media Lab study found negative content reaches 50% of its total potential audience within 90 minutes of publication. These two facts together create a brutal math: the window to get ahead of a narrative is 60–90 minutes.

### Four-Phase Crisis Response `[STABLE]`
1. **Detect** — Social listening tools (Brand24, Sprout Social, Mention) with real-time alerts. Set threshold: any sentiment spike >2σ above 7-day baseline triggers review.
2. **Acknowledge (≤60 min)** — Publish a holding statement: "We're aware of [issue], we take it seriously, we're investigating, we'll have more in [X hours]." Do not speculate on cause. Do not minimize. Do not go silent.
3. **Respond (≤4 hr)** — Root cause (as known), corrective action taken, stakeholder impact statement, empathy-first language. Have a named spokesperson. Source: Xpoz AI 2025.
4. **Recover** — Post-crisis: third-party validation (earned coverage of the resolution), internal alignment, lessons-documented update, rebuild SoV/sentiment baseline over 30–90 days.

### Pre-Built Templates Are Not Optional `[STABLE]`
Brands that meet the 60-min standard consistently use pre-approved holding statement templates. The holding statement template needs: [brand name] + [issue category e.g. product, employment, data, environment] + [empathy phrase] + [action being taken] + [timeline for next update]. Review and update templates quarterly. Approvals must be pre-cleared — a crisis is not the time to chase legal sign-off for a boilerplate paragraph.

### Empathy-First Principle `[STABLE]`
70% of consumers say a brand simply acknowledging a problem within the first hour significantly reduces their frustration — even before a resolution is offered (Sprout Social Index 2025). The implication: acknowledgment alone is a meaningful crisis management action. The two most trust-destroying behaviors are silence and defensiveness. Empathy is not spin — it is the acknowledgment of human impact before corporate positioning.

### Sentiment Recovery Delta `[VOLATILE]`
Brands responding within 2 hours see 61% better sentiment recovery compared to delayed responses (Xpoz AI 2025). This is the single most action-relevant number in crisis comms. Sentiment recovery is measurable via social listening tools post-response: track positive/negative/neutral ratio at 24 hr, 72 hr, and 7 days post-incident. A recovery arc should show negative sentiment declining from peak within 24–72 hr if response was effective.

## 3. Brand Voice + Messaging House

### Message House Architecture `[STABLE]`
The message house has three layers:
- **Roof** — One core narrative: the single most important thing the world should believe about this company. All communications derive from it. If you can't say it in one sentence, it isn't finished.
- **Walls** — 3–5 messaging pillars: the recurring themes that prove the core narrative from different angles (e.g., product innovation, team expertise, market category creation, customer impact, mission). Each pillar should map to a stakeholder group.
- **Foundation** — Proof points: specific facts, case studies, customer quotes, data that give journalists, investors, and customers a concrete reason to believe each pillar. Without proof points, pillars are claims. With proof points, they are evidence.

Source: Branding Journal 2025; Asana Brand Messaging Framework 2026; Reforge; Fabrik Brands.

### Voice vs. Tone `[STABLE]`
Voice is the brand's personality — consistent across all channels and content types. Tone is how that voice modulates depending on context: a brand with a "bold, direct" voice might use a softer, more empathetic tone in customer service and a sharper, more provocative tone in advertising. The test: a customer should recognize the brand as the same entity whether reading a tweet, a press release, or a support email.

### Consistency Enforcement `[STABLE]`
Common enforcement tools: brand voice guide (adjectives + do/don't examples), message house one-pager distributed to all external-facing functions, a copy review checklist against pillars before any press release, executive speech, or social post goes live. Inconsistency across channels is the fastest way to undermine earned media credibility — journalists who see different core claims across touchpoints assume the company doesn't know its own story.

## 4. Social Listening / Reputation

### Core Monitoring KPIs `[VOLATILE — platform weighting shifts quarterly]`
- **Share of Voice (SoV)**: brand's conversation share vs. named competitors in the same category. The North Star competitive metric.
- **Sentiment ratio**: positive:negative:neutral breakdown. Track trend, not point-in-time snapshot. A -2σ drop in positive sentiment over 7 days is an early crisis signal.
- **Mention volume**: absolute count; useful for measuring announcement lift and baseline awareness.
- **Reach/impressions**: audience size of mentions, weighted by publication authority.
- **Response rate and time**: how quickly the brand acknowledges @mentions, especially on X (Twitter) and Instagram.
- **Emotional sentiment**: LLM-powered tools now distinguish beyond positive/negative into trust, surprise, anger, anticipation. GPT-4-class models achieve 93% precision on binary sentiment tasks (Brand24 2026); multi-emotion precision lower, ~70–80%.

### Alert Threshold Design `[STABLE]`
Set rolling 7-day baseline for mention volume and sentiment ratio. Alert triggers: (a) volume spike >3σ in 1 hour, (b) negative sentiment ratio crossing 30% of daily mentions, (c) any single mention achieving >50K impressions. These thresholds are configurable per brand size; adjust multipliers for smaller brands with lower baseline volumes.

### AI-Powered Listening Tools (2026 Landscape) `[VOLATILE]`
Tier-1 platforms: Sprout Social, Brandwatch, Brand24, Mention, Meltwater. Key 2026 differentiator is LLM-powered sentiment and topic classification that surfaces emerging themes before they become crises. Platform selection criterion: real-time alert latency (sub-15-minute detection is table stakes for enterprise), cross-channel coverage (news + social + forums + video), and sentiment model accuracy.

## 5. Announcement / Launch Comms

### Embargo Strategy `[STABLE]`
**Embargo** (broad simultaneous release): send to all target journalists 2–3 weeks before launch date under embargo. Gives journalists time to write substantive pieces that publish simultaneously. Best for: product launches, funding rounds, major hires. Risk: embargo breaks (rare but possible; pre-screen journalists before including).

**Exclusive** (single outlet, head start): offer one top-tier outlet the story ahead of all others, on the condition they publish first. In return, the story gets prominent placement. Best for: complex narratives that benefit from long-form treatment, or when you want to establish a frame before the broader press cycle.

**Hybrid**: exclusives to tier-1 followed by broad embargo lift. Requires careful sequencing and clear embargo timing language to avoid conflict.

### Launch Timing Anti-Patterns `[STABLE]`
- Do NOT launch during Apple's September iPhone event, CES, or Mobile World Congress — your story is swallowed by category gravity.
- Do NOT launch on a Friday — newsrooms thin; web traffic lower.
- Do NOT launch the same week as a major competitor announcement if avoidable.
- DO use Tuesday–Thursday mornings as the default window.
- DO send at off-the-hour times (e.g., 10:12 am) to reduce inbox competition.

### Multi-Channel Launch Sequencing `[STABLE]`
Day -21 to -14: embargo outreach to journalist shortlist. Day -7: confirm publication plans with each journalist. Day 0, 6 am ET: embargo lifts simultaneously; owned channels go live (blog, social, email). Day 0, 8 am–12 pm: founder/CEO LinkedIn post, executive quotes amplified via personal profiles. Day 0–3: paid amplification on top earned coverage. Day 7: first performance read (pickup count, SoV change, sentiment baseline shift).

## 6. Founder Thought Leadership

### Why Personal Profile Beats Company Page `[VOLATILE — platform algorithm; quarterly drift]`
LinkedIn's feed algorithm allocates ~65% of feed impressions to personal profiles and ~5% to company pages (2026 estimates, Linkboost / Digital Applied). Personal profiles generate 8× more engagement than company pages (Rosica 2026). The implication: a founder's personal LinkedIn presence is a higher-ROI owned channel than the company's brand page. This is a structural platform dynamic, not a content quality issue.

### 2026 LinkedIn Depth Score Update `[VOLATILE]`
LinkedIn introduced a "Depth Score" ranking signal in 2026 that weights time-spent-engaging over surface clicks and reactions. Posts that hold attention (long-form, native video, document carousels) rank higher than link-out posts. LinkedIn's detection models actively suppress AI-generated content that lacks a distinct human point of view or follows standard AI structural patterns. The implication: founder content must have a clear, differentiated opinion — not a balanced "on the one hand / on the other hand" structure.

### Thought Leadership Purchase Influence `[VOLATILE]`
95% of B2B decision-makers report that thought leadership directly influences their purchasing decisions (Edelman-LinkedIn B2B TL Impact Report 2025/26). 64% of buyers prefer thought leadership over product/promo content. 63% say TL content is important for demonstrating that an organization understands their specific business challenges. This makes founder TL a direct pipeline tool, not a brand-awareness vanity play.

### Optimal Frequency and Content Mix `[VOLATILE]`
- 1–2 posts per week is the optimal frequency for most founders/CEOs. Consistency outperforms burst-then-silence patterns.
- Content that performs: specific opinions on industry shifts, lessons from operational experience, reaction to breaking industry news with a named point of view, data the audience cannot find elsewhere.
- Content that underperforms: inspirational platitudes, reposted company news without personal framing, unedited AI-generated text, engagement-bait questions with no substance.

### Thought Leadership Authority Ladder `[STABLE]`
Stage 1 — Niche POV: consistent posting in one domain; build a small but highly relevant following. Stage 2 — Trade Media: bylined op-eds in vertical publications (credibility signal; links for SEO). Stage 3 — Tier-1 Business Press: Forbes/WSJ/FT features or quotes as named expert. Stage 4 — Keynote + Book: summit keynotes and published books create durable authority assets that compound for years. Progress up the ladder requires documentary proof at each stage (clip file, speaking reel, named coverage).
