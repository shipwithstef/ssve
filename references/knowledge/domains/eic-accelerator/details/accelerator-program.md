# EIC Accelerator — Program Mechanics

## Mechanism

**Purpose (verbatim):** support to start-ups and SMEs that "have a innovative, game changing product, service or business model that could create new markets or disrupt existing ones... have the ambition and commitment to scale up... but the risks involved are too high for private investors alone to invest."

**Funding forms:**
- **Grant component:** "below EUR 2.5 million" for innovation activities **TRL 6-8**. Lump-sum based (Type 2) since 2026.
- **Investment component:** "€1 - €10 million" (work programme range €0.5-10M) via the **EIC Fund**, for activities to **TRL 9** / market scale-up. Maximum requestable amount under WP2026 = **€10M** (above that → STEP Scale Up, €10-30M).
- **Modes:** blended finance (grant + investment), grant-only, grant-first, equity-only. Since WP2026 there are "no preconditions to apply for equity only support" (previously required a prior grant).
- **Grant-only applicants** must demonstrate they have own access to commercialisation/scale-up resources, and must still describe market activities.

**Who can apply:**
- Single start-up or SME (including spin-outs) from EU Member States or Horizon Europe associated countries
- Small mid-caps (up to 499 employees) — **investment only**
- Natural persons or legal entities willing to set up an SME or mid-cap
- Third-country applicants: may submit a short proposal but "must prove its effective establishment in a Member State or an Associated Country at the time of submission of the full proposal"
- UK applicants: **grant-only scheme only** (no blended/equity; no follow-up EIC equity for UK grant-only holders)
- Concurrent applications: only ONE application at a time; next application only after results of the prior one

**TRL eligibility floor:** "at least all activities for TRL 5, i.e. 'Validation in relevant environment', has been achieved." For GenAI: TRL 5 = system tested/validated in simulated or lab environment closely mimicking real-world conditions with real datasets. Clinical-trials mapping: TRL 5 = animal testing + phase-1 protocols; TRL 6 = exploratory phase 1; TRL 7 = phase 2 completed; TRL 8 = phase 3 completed + regulatory approval.

**Open vs Challenges:**
- **EIC Accelerator Open** — no predefined topic: "innovations in any field of technology and innovations that cut across different scientific, technological, sectoral and application fields." 2026 budget: **€414 million**.
- **EIC Accelerator Challenges** — predefined strategic topics, separate reserved budgets, challenge-specific juries and portfolios. 2026 budget: **€220 million** (per-challenge €20-50M). See `accelerator-challenges-2025-2026.md`.
- EISMEA never moves proposals between Open and a Challenge — the applicant's choice is binding (except obvious clerical errors via helpdesk).
- Some Challenges carry **ownership-control restrictions** (e.g. Critical Raw Materials): grant-only applicants file an ownership control declaration; control by non-eligible third countries → ineligible. Open-call proposals falling in that scope must file the same declaration if selected.

**2026 budgets and evolution:** Accelerator total 2026 = **€634M**. WP2026 changes: full-proposal form cut from 50 → 20 pages; "evaluations every 2 months instead of every 6 months"; in-depth tech assessment during evaluation "anticipating the due diligence needed for investments."

## Analysis

- The Accelerator is a **deep-tech instrument**: the de facto bar is proprietary technology at TRL 5+ with market-creating potential, single-company (no consortium), heavy emphasis on financials (4-year balance-sheet forecast required).
- The grant/investment split is structural: grant money cannot fund TRL 9 / market deployment; investment can fund anything (including pre-TRL9 innovation work packages if flagged for the investment component — this also keeps grant-agreement duration shorter).
- Jury cannot switch your funding form (e.g. blended → grant-only) but CAN lower the grant amount (e.g. strip TRL9 activities) and comment on the investment amount.
- Budget is split across the year's batches deliberately — applying at an "unpopular" cut-off does not improve odds materially; EIC advises applying when ready.
