# EIC Fund — Investment Component Mechanics

## Mechanism

**Structure & governance:**
- The **EIC Fund** is "an alternative investment fund (AIF) for investing in companies selected by the EIC Accelerator." An appointed external **AIFM** (alternative investment fund manager) takes all investment decisions (since Autumn 2022). The **EIB** is "the unique investment adviser" — advises and runs due diligence but does NOT decide. Since 1 Jan 2024 the Commission's EIC Fund shares are temporarily held by the EIB on the Commission's behalf with safeguards.
- EISMEA keeps evaluation, grant agreements, and grant↔equity coordination. Investments are authorised by a Commission **Award Decision** (sets a maximum investment amount = requested amount + flexibility margin of up to **€2M** for Accelerator / **€5M** for STEP).
- "Impact investment" objective, patient capital; exit case-by-case: IPOs, management buyouts, secondary sales, liquidations.

**Investment process after a jury GO:**
1. Application data goes to the EIB shortly after the interview; an EIC investment officer contacts the company to gauge urgency.
2. KYC + initial assessment → the EIB categorises the case into **investment scenarios ("buckets")** based on investment maturity and ability to attract co-investments.
3. Time-critical rounds get prioritised due diligence; companies without short/medium-term fundraising plans may proceed with the grant and defer investment DD.
4. Blended-finance investments "must take place no later than 12 months following the end date of the grant."
5. The Fund MAY reject an investment on substantial negative DD findings even after a jury GO.

**Instruments used:**
- **Direct equity** when co-investors show immediate interest — the Fund seeks the round at-least-matched by others ("at least 50% of the round"), targeting ≥1:3 leverage at portfolio level and ≥1:1 matching per investment.
- **Convertibles/quasi-equity** (loans/bonds/notes, participation rights, **SAFEs**) for companies "not yet sufficiently mature to attract other investors" — an exceptional bridge with the expectation of a qualified priced round later. No fixed test for "sufficiently tried but failed to find an investor"; case-by-case.
- Valuation aligns to the market (the private qualified co-investors' terms).

**Qualified investor (verbatim):** "an investor deploying privately sourced funds in an EIC Fund Final Recipient with demonstrable know-how and experience in the relevant market, technology and jurisdiction." Ideal leads: institutional VC/growth funds with sector experience, specialist deep-tech investors, professional family offices on market terms. Public/semi-public bodies, strategic CVCs with commercial objectives, policy-mandate entities can participate but are scrutinised as leads. "New investor" ≈ not previously on the cap table and unaffiliated — independent market validation and price discovery.

**Tech due diligence:** performed by the EIB-coordinated external expert (full tech DD for STEP); launched right after evaluation/selection; runs parallel to negotiation ("does not slow down the Investment process"); complements rather than re-assesses the evaluation; expert bound by Horizon Europe impartiality/confidentiality contract; no formal validity period — revisited on material changes.

**Economic security / strategic safeguards:** for AI, semiconductors, quantum, biotech the Commission case-by-case assesses risk of non-EU acquisition; possible measures in the investment agreement include HQ-stays-in-Europe during the investment period. Strategic-tech companies also get follow-on support: larger STEP investments, experienced non-executive directors. The Fund ensures "supported companies keep most of their value, including their IP, in the EU or in the Associated Countries."

**Other key rules:**
- Max requestable Accelerator investment: **€10M** (WP2026); €10-30M only via STEP.
- Equity-only: no preconditions under WP2026 (previously needed eligible prior grant). H2020 grant-only pilot beneficiaries may apply for investment; ongoing grant-only Accelerator projects may go via Fast Track.
- No geographic exclusion of co-investors a priori, but economic-security review in strategic areas; company consent required before the Fund engages other investors.
- **Grant First**: to unlock the equity component, the Key Commercial/Co-investment Milestone OR Key Technological Milestone must be achieved "no later than six months before the end of the project"; afterwards the Term Sheet has an expiry date but no formal investment deadline.
- No guaranteed minimum first-disbursement percentage; staged investments are possible but not default.
- InvestEU boundary: EIC funds "non-bankable" companies; once bankable they graduate to InvestEU. EIC Fund equity is NOT repayable like a loan; EU support cannot repay other EU support. Partial InvestEU coverage still counts as non-bankable; EIC investment may de-risk the round.
- Board seats: investment agreement may give the Fund a director-appointment right; EICF directors are non-executive, owe fiduciary duties to the company, selected from a March-2024 public call list; not limited to EU nationals (subject to local company law).
- IPR: the Fund "does not seek to own or control companies' intellectual property" but may impose proportionate IP protection/transfer conditions.
- EIC Fund Memorandum is internal; applicants rely on the public **EIC Fund Investment Guidelines**, WP2026, and HE Model Grant Agreement.

## Analysis

- The equity component is structurally a **co-investment catalyst**, not a standalone VC: without a private qualified investor the path degrades to convertibles/SAFE bridges. Founders should treat "find a qualified lead" as part of the EIC application work.
- The 12-month post-grant deadline for blended investments and the Grant-First 6-month milestone rule are the two timing traps that kill investment components.
- For the builder's planning: EIC equity arrives slowly (DD after interview, negotiation, syndication) — it is runway for scaling, not seed survival money.
